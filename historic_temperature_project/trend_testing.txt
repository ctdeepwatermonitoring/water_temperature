Documentation of trend tests and visualizations

Documentation of processes mann_kendall.R:

Required libraries:
tidyverse
broom
ggforce
Kendall
patchwork
scales
EnvStats

Helpers and constants:
COLD_MAX is set to 18.29, the maximum value considered to be cold water for fish communities.
WARM_MIN is set to 21.70, the minimum value considered to be warm water for fish communities.

THRESHOLD_LINES_LEGEND is a tibble used as labels for the plots.
THRESHOLD_COLORS defines the colors used in the charts for each threshold.
THRESHOLD_LINETYPES sets solid lines to use in the plots.

clean_sites is a function that takes a dataframe, standardizes STA_SEQ as a character, and selects it as well as the WaterbodyName.

theil_sen is a function that computes theil-sen slopes given two numeric variables x and y.

build_mk_label is a function that creates labels from a dataframe using tau, slope, and p-value.


Initial Steps:
The temperature data is read in from the csv along with the list of sites and a landscape cover file.
Temp data is cleaned with clean_sites.
Daily mean temperatures are calculated for each site on a certain date, and sites are joined to the waterbody name.


Mann-Kendall for Summer Temperature Trend Steps:
Mean or median can be selected interchangeably as the desired method for average at the beginning of any of the Mann-Kendall tests in this file.
Average temperatures for summer are calculated for each site each year from the daily means.
Using the Kendall package, a Mann-Kendall trend test is conducted on temperature and year for sites with 10 or more years of data.
The averages are joined to the results of the Mann-Kendall test and an intercept is calculated.
A summary is created grouped by station id and waterbody name, including number of years of data, the minimum and maximum year, the minimum and maximum average temp, tau, p-value, theil-sen slope, and intercept.
Labels are created and the sites are split into facets and plotted.
Threshold lines are decided upon based upon the range of the data present.
Pages consisting of 3x3 scatter plots will be produced containing the year on the x-axis and summer averages on the y-axis, connected by a line over time.
Threshold lines are drawn for comparison, and Theil-Sen slopes are shown with a trendline.


Seasonal Mann-Kendall Trend Test for Summer Months Steps:
Mean or median can be selected interchangeably as the desired method for average at the beginning of any of the Mann-Kendall tests in this file.
Average temperatures for each summer month are calculated individually for each site each year from the daily means.
Using the EnvStats package, a Seasonal Mann-Kendall trend test is conducted on temperature and year for sites with 10 or more years of data, using each of the three summer months as seasons.
The averages are joined to the results of the Seasonal Mann-Kendall test and an intercept is calculated.
A summary is created grouped by station id and waterbody name, including number of years of data, the minimum and maximum year, the minimum and maximum average temp, tau, p-value, theil-sen slope, and intercept.
Labels are created and the sites are split into facets and plotted.
Threshold lines are decided upon based upon the range of the data present.
Pages consisting of 3x3 scatter plots will be produced containing the year on the x-axis and summer averages on the y-axis, connected by a line over time.
Three dots can be expected per year for sites with all summer months of data.
Threshold lines are drawn for comparison, and Theil-Sen slopes are shown with a trendline.


Seasonal Mann-Kendall Trend Test for Meteorological Seasons Steps:
Mean or median can be selected interchangeably as the desired method for average at the beginning of any of the Mann-Kendall tests in this file.
Average temperatures for each meteorological season are calculated individually for each site each year from the daily means.
Using the EnvStats package, a Seasonal Mann-Kendall trend test is conducted on temperature and year for sites with 10 or more years of data, using each of the four meteorological seasons as seasons.
The averages are joined to the results of the Seasonal Mann-Kendall test and an intercept is calculated.
A summary is created grouped by station id and waterbody name, including number of years of data, the minimum and maximum year, the minimum and maximum average temp, tau, p-value, theil-sen slope, and intercept.
Labels are created and the sites are split into facets and plotted.
Threshold lines are decided upon based upon the range of the data present.
Pages consisting of 3x3 scatter plots will be produced containing the year on the x-axis and season averages on the y-axis, connected by a line over time.
Four dots can be expected per year for sites with all seasons of data.
Threshold lines are drawn for comparison, and Theil-Sen slopes are shown with a trendline.
A comparison heatmap is also drawn to show how many years of data each season has for each site.


Seasonal Mann-Kendall Trend Test for Bioperiods Steps:
Mean or median can be selected interchangeably as the desired method for average at the beginning of any of the Mann-Kendall tests in this file.
Average temperatures for each bioperiod are calculated individually for each site each year from the daily means.
Using the EnvStats package, a Seasonal Mann-Kendall trend test is conducted on temperature and year for sites with 10 or more years of data, using each of the six bioperiods as seasons.
The averages are joined to the results of the Seasonal Mann-Kendall test and an intercept is calculated.
A summary is created grouped by station id and waterbody name, including number of years of data, the minimum and maximum year, the minimum and maximum average temp, tau, p-value, theil-sen slope, and intercept.
Labels are created and the sites are split into facets and plotted.
Threshold lines are decided upon based upon the range of the data present.
Pages consisting of 3x3 scatter plots will be produced containing the year on the x-axis and bioperiod averages on the y-axis, connected by a line over time.
Six dots can be expected per year for sites with all bioperiods of data.
Threshold lines are drawn for comparison, and Theil-Sen slopes are shown with a trendline.
A comparison heatmap is also drawn to show how many years of data each bioperiod has for each site.


Steps for Comparisons of Significant Values Across Tests:
Results for each testing method are given a column indicating the test and then filtered to only those sites with a p-value of less thna 0.1.
Rows of the results are bound and a pivot table is created which shows the test method, the site, and the p-value.
A tile plot is created for ease of viewing.
The bound dataframe is filtered to p-values < 0.05 and the process is repeated.





Documentation of bar_charts.R:

Required libraries:
tidyverse
broom
ggforce
patchwork

Helpers and constants:
temp_category is a function that assigns Cold, Cool, and Warm temperature categories based on the temperature thresholds.

COLD_MAX is set to 18.29, the maximum value considered to be cold water for fish communities.
WARM_MIN is set to 21.70, the minimum value considered to be warm water for fish communities.

THRESHOLD_LINES_LEGEND is a tibble used as labels for the plots.
THRESHOLD_COLORS defines the colors used in the charts for each threshold.
THRESHOLD_LINETYPES sets solid lines to use in the plots.

clean_sites is a function that takes a dataframe, standardizes STA_SEQ as a character, and selects it as well as the WaterbodyName.


Initial Steps:
The temperature data is read in from the csv along with the list of sites and a landscape cover file.
Temperature data is cleaned with clean_sites.
Daily mean temperatures are calculated for each site on a certain date, and sites are joined to the waterbody name.
The summer averages are calculated by filtering the data down to June-August, grouping by site, year, and waterbody name, then calculating mean.
Temperature category is assigned.

Steps for bar charts:
Sites with less than 10 years of data are filtered out.
Data is given a year group of a 5 year range (ex. 2000 - 2004, 2015-2019, etc.).
Means and medians are calculated for the year groups.
Mean or median can be selected interchangeably as the desired method for average.
A summary is produced grouped by site showing the minimum and maximum temperatures.
Threshold lines are decided upon based upon the range of the data present.
Pages consisting of 3x3 column plots are created with the 5-year period on the x-axis and the average summer temperature on the y-axis.
The number of years in each 5-year period are displayed over their columns.





Documentation of timeline_heatmaps.R:

Required libraries:
tidyverse
broom
ggforce
patchwork

Helpers and constants:
temp_category is a function that assigns Cold, Cool, and Warm temperature categories based on the temperature thresholds (<18.29 for Cold Water, >18.29 and <21.70 for Cool Water, and >21.70 for Warm Water).

clean_sites is a function that takes a dataframe, standardizes STA_SEQ as a character, and selects it as well as the WaterbodyName.

Initial Steps:
The temperature data is read in from the csv along with the list of sites and a landscape cover file.
Temperature data is cleaned with clean_sites.
Daily mean temperatures are calculated for each site on a certain date, and sites are joined to the waterbody name.

Steps to produce heatmap of temperature categories in a specific year:
A target year is first declared.
Daily means are filtered down to the target year and only for June-August.
The top 30 sites with the most coverage are calculated.
The daily means are further filtered down to the top 30 sites by coverage.
A heatmap is created with the date range from June 1 through August 31.
The top 30 sites are displayed as well as their daily mean temperature categories over the summer.

Steps to produce heatmap of summer temperature categories over all years:
Daily means are filtered down to June-August, grouped by site and year, and mean temperatures are calculated.
Temperature categories are assigned (Cold, Cool, and Warm).
The top 30 sites with the most years of data are calculated.
A heatmap is created ranging across all years for which there are data.
The top 30 sites are displayed as well as their mean temperature categories over the summer.
A continuous heatmap is also made that plots the same information but with actual temperature values instead of categories.

Misc plots/processes:
Summer average temperatures are calculated.
Top 4 sites by amount of summer coverage are calculated.
A dot plot with a line connecting the points and a trendline is produced to show the temperature change over time.

The same process is done but for the overall summer temperature trend across all sites.

Regex can be used to determine Waterbody type.

Slopes of all longterm sites for summer averages are calculated.
The trendline for the sites with the highest 5 slopes and their data points are plotted as the most extreme summer temperatures.
